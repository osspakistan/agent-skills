# Pattern Library

Patterns are ordered by corpus support, strongest first. Each lists the structure, typical length, the moves that make it feel authentic, and the real emails it was distilled from (by subject/date) so you can reason about the source. Do not copy those emails; write original text for the user's situation.

Contents
- Tier 1: rally, cost control, culture directive, layoffs/reorg, policy edict, crisis all-hands
- Tier 2: terse reply, delegation, decline, setback-and-resolve, celebration
- Tier 3: persuasive rationale, board/investor, customer announcement, press reply, business outreach
- Tier 4: extrapolation guide

---

## TIER 1

### 1. Rally / deadline push
**Length:** 2 to 6 sentences.
**Structure:** (1) state where we stand and what is at stake, (2) the specific ask ("go all out", "rally hard"), (3) one concrete obstacle or priority, (4) offer of direct help, (5) short thanks.
**Moves:**
- Name the exact finish line: days left, a target number, a record to beat.
- Admit when it's tight ("looking super tight") rather than pretending confidence.
- Say why this email exists: a line like "I would only send this note if it really mattered" signals rarity.
- Add a "tell me directly" escape hatch for people who feel unheard.
**Sources:** end-of-quarter emails of Jun 2019, Sep 2019, Jun 2020, Sep 2020, Dec 2020 ("Five days to 500,000"), Jun 2021; "Ignore all distractions" (Sep 2018); "We did it!!" (Jul 2018).

**Adaptation example (original):**
> Subject: Last five days
>
> We are 340 orders short of our best month ever, and we have five days to close it. Every shipment that leaves the dock before Friday counts; everything after counts for next month. Please treat packing and delivery as the top priority until then, whatever your title. If you see a bottleneck, email me directly and I will help clear it.
>
> Thanks for the hard work,
> [Your name]

### 2. Cost control / spending discipline
**Length:** 200 to 400 words.
**Structure:** (1) why careful spending still matters now, (2) the numbers (burn, margin, runway), (3) the principle: many small savings add up, (4) the mechanism (who reviews what), (5) acknowledge it's hardcore and may take weeks to get right, (6) the mission link, (7) thanks.
**Moves:**
- Do the arithmetic out loud: unit cost times volume equals real money.
- A vivid pennies image ("a game of pennies") makes small savings feel important.
- Make the control mechanism personal and specific (an approver signing every payment page, and the sender signing every tenth one).
- Give permission to be imperfect at first.
**Sources:** "Hardcore control of expenses" (May 2019), "Game of Pennies" (Dec 2020), the Profit section of "Progress, Precision and Profit" (Apr 2018), "Minimize delivery cost" (Nov 2021), "Save costs or we are doomed" (Feb 2016, internal and blunt).

### 3. Culture / productivity directive
**Length:** 150 to 450 words.
**Structure:** (1) the problem in one blunt sentence, (2) a dash-led list of rules, each with a one-line reason, (3) a "use common sense" release valve, (4) quick thanks.
**Moves:**
- Rules are anti-bureaucratic: cut big meetings, leave calls where you add no value, skip acronyms, skip chain of command.
- Give consequences in one flat sentence ("will soon find themselves working elsewhere").
- End with a common-sense override: if a rule is ridiculous, change the rule.
- Show you apply it to yourself ("I still do not have my own office").
**Sources:** "Acronyms Seriously Suck" (May 2010), "Communication Within Tesla" (Aug 2017), productivity bullets in the Apr 2018 memo, "Please Note" (Oct 2021), no management offices (2017), "Music in the Factory" (Oct 2021, the warm counterpart).

### 4. Layoffs / reorganization
**Length:** 250 to 700 words (shorter, ~50 words, for a pure headcount notice).
**Structure:** (1) state the decision and its size, (2) the reason (duplicated roles, cost, sustainability), (3) who is and isn't affected, (4) sincere thanks to those leaving and what they receive, (5) clarity and reassurance for those staying, (6) a resolve line ("we are doing this now so we never have to again"), (7) thanks.
**Moves:**
- Say the hard number early and plainly.
- Name the unfair-but-true criticism and accept it ("a valid and fair criticism").
- Include at least one line about the people leaving that sounds like he means it.
- Close with the mission and the work ahead.
**Sources:** "Reorg Update" (Jun 2018), "Company Update" 7% layoff (Jan 2019), "Headcount Reduction" (Jun 2022, very short), more than 10% reduction (Apr 2024), store-closure clarification "seeds on barren ground" (Mar 2019, a follow-up that corrects fears with a three-bucket rule).
**Add:** one-line HR/legal check suggestion for the user.

### 5. Policy edict (return to office and similar)
**Length:** 60 to 200 words; often followed by a second "To be super clear" email.
**Structure:** (1) the rule with a number ("minimum, and I mean *minimum*, of 40 hours"), (2) comparison that makes it feel fair, (3) the exception path (approved personally), (4) consequence, (5) a pride sentence about what the company makes.
**Moves:**
- Anchor fairness in a comparison ("this is less than we ask of factory workers").
- Seniority leads by example: the more senior, the more visible.
- A follow-up that restates the rule even more bluntly is authentic.
**Sources:** Tesla and SpaceX return-to-office emails (May 2022), Twitter's first all-company email (Nov 2022), "A fork in the road" (Nov 2022), exceptions-if-a-manager-vouches follow-ups.
**Add:** HR/legal check line.

### 6. Crisis all-hands
**Length:** 150 to 350 words.
**Structure:** (1) the situation is worse than reported, no sugarcoating, (2) what the sender is personally doing (on the line all weekend), (3) the ask (all hands, specifics on who is exempt), (4) the chain of consequences if we fail, in plain business terms, (5) the threshold for survival, (6) thanks.
**Moves:**
- The sender shows up physically or personally first, then asks the same of others.
- Spell out the dependency chain: this part fails, therefore that product fails, therefore this financial risk.
- Give an exact threshold ("a flight every two weeks next year").
**Sources:** "Raptor production crisis" (Nov 2021), "Some concerning news" (Jun 2018, the sabotage email; note it contains an unproven accusation, so don't model the accusation), "Doing the right thing" (May 2017).

## TIER 2

### 7. Terse reply
**Length:** 1 to 12 words.
**Examples of the register:** "Sounds good", "Ok by me", "Absolutely", "Great", "Will call", "Yes", "Agreed".
**Moves:**
- Answer exactly the question asked and stop.
- If a short condition matters, state it in one clause ("Fine by me if they don't use this in active messaging").
- Never pad with pleasantries.

### 8. Delegation
**Length:** 1 to 3 sentences.
**Pattern:** the approval, then a name and an imperative: "Consider it done! [Name], please facilitate. Let's try to [specific outcome]."
**Moves:** direct address by first name, a concrete target ("slot these four cars into next month's production"), a collaborative "Let's".

### 9. Decline
**Length:** 2 to 3 sentences.
**Pattern:** brief appreciation, flat reason ("my obligations prevent me"), alternative on his terms ("let's schedule when he's next in town"), and a downgrade of urgency ("this is not an urgent matter"). For bigger invitations, he states what he actually does with his time and calls the trip "an unwise use of time".
**Moves:** no apology spiral; the reason is about priorities, not feelings.

### 10. Setback and resolve
**Length:** 150 to 250 words.
**Structure:** (1) say it was a big disappointment, (2) the one thing that went right, (3) the problem in one honest sentence plus "I will send a note when we know", (4) "we will not skip a beat" with the next concrete steps, (5) a funding or resourcing reassurance if relevant, (6) "I will never give up".
**Sources:** Falcon 1 Flight 3 "Plan Going Forward" (Aug 2008), "Why SpaceX should stay private" 2013 for tone on setbacks.

### 11. Celebration and thanks
**Length:** 60 to 200 words.
**Moves:** open with the achievement in his own excited voice ("We did it!!"), name specific feats (tents, cargo planes, new lines), one understated big line ("I think we just became a real car company"), honest thanks. In quieter variants, say that the work is "pure goodness" and that you respect the people doing it more than the richest person on Wall Street.
**Sources:** "7000" (Jul 2018), "Pure goodness" (Jun 2022), Model 3 "2,000 per week" (Apr 2018).

## TIER 3

### 12. Persuasive rationale memo
**Length:** 600 to 1,500 words.
**Structure:** (1) the position in two sentences, (2) the underlying principle, (3) objections handled as all-caps labels ("YES, BUT I COULD MAKE MORE MONEY"), (4) the plan, the choice offered, and what stays the same, (5) the numbers, (6) clear advice ("my recommendation"), (7) an aspirational close.
**Moves:**
- Anticipate the smart skeptic and answer them directly, with specifics.
- Use a short vivid contrast (public company stocks as "manic-depressive").
- Give a number-based sense of the stakes ("twelve flights per year to hit 10% profitability").
- Offer the reader a decision rather than an order, then recommend one.
**Sources:** "Going Public" (Jun 2013), "Taking Tesla private" (Aug 2018), the SpaceX/xAI "next book" memo (Feb 2026), "Importance of Options" price explanation (Jan 2009).

### 13. Board / investor email
**Length:** 50 to 300 words.
**Structure:** numbered reasons, a clear next step ("put to a shareholder vote at the earliest opportunity"), an expiry ("this offer expires in 30 days"), and respect for those who choose differently.
**Moves:** crisp enumerated logic, deadline, no hedging. For tense counterpart messages, state the condition for continuing ("I will not take the call unless X is corrected"). Keep it about conduct, not character.
**Sources:** "Offer to Take Tesla Private at $420" (Aug 2018), Twitter offer letter (Apr 2022: short, mission-first, price, "best and final"), PIF and Goldman emails (Aug 2018), shareholder-vote tactics (Sep 2016).

### 14. Customer / owner announcement
**Length:** 250 to 500 words.
**Structure:** (1) a personal opening about what customers tell him, (2) honest explanation of the economics, (3) the offer or change in concrete terms, (4) "just for fun" extras, (5) short sign-off.
**Moves:** candor about cost and mistakes (an estimate "wrong by a very large margin"), framing as an experiment, perks that money can't buy.
**Sources:** referral program (Jul 2015), Roadster option price increase (Jan 2009), no-discount integrity email (Sep 2016).

### 15. Reply to a journalist
**Length:** 2 to 6 sentences.
**Moves:** challenge the premise with a number or a fact, offer a clear counter-offer ("if they ever show up with a vehicle that qualifies, we will accommodate them"), allow one memorable image. Keep it about the claim, not the reporter.
**Sources:** Blue Origin/LC-39A reply (Sep 2013), autopilot "do the bloody math" (Jul 2016), NPR label exchange (Apr 2023, short and combative).
**Warning:** the corpus also holds hostile and defamatory press emails (the 2018 BuzzFeed exchange). Never model those: no insults, no accusations about a person's private life, and no "off the record" tricks.

### 16. Business outreach / ask
**Length:** 2 to 5 sentences.
**Structure:** "Hi [Name]," + the direct request, + one clarifying "Worth flagging: ..." line that heads off a misunderstanding, + "Thanks".
**Sources:** "DGX-1 for OpenAI" to Nvidia's CEO (Apr 2016).

## TIER 4: Extrapolation guide

For emails the corpus doesn't cover, apply these defaults and add the extrapolation note:

| Email type | Adapt the voice by... |
|---|---|
| Apology | Short, plain accountability: what happened, what I own, what changes, by when. No groveling. (The corpus has almost no apologies, so keep it simple.) |
| Condolences / personal | Drop the intensity entirely. Keep it short, sincere, and specific. No humor, no mission talk. |
| Cold sales / recruiting | Lead with the specific ask or offer and one hard number; make the ask easy to say no to. Avoid hype adjectives. |
| Customer support | Own the problem, say exactly what happens next and when. Cut apologies to one sentence. |
| Formal/legal | Use his plain-spoken structure inside a safer, more neutral register. Recommend professional review. |
| Newsletter / announcement | Treat as the customer-announcement pattern (#14). |
| Thank-you / referral | Treat as celebration (#11), shorter. |
