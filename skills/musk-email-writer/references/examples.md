# Worked Examples

One original example per pattern in `patterns.md`, ordered Tier 1 to Tier 4. These are written for invented companies and are **not** real Musk emails. Each ends with "Notice" (what makes it work) and a "Real echo" (a very short fragment from the source corpus, to show the register).

Use these to calibrate length, rhythm, and structure. Do not copy their wording or their metaphors into a user's email; write fresh text for the user's situation.

Contents
- Tier 1: rally, cost control, culture directive, layoffs, policy edict, crisis
- Tier 2: terse replies, delegation, decline, setback, celebration
- Tier 3: rationale memo, board/investor, customer announcement, press reply, outreach
- Tier 4: apology, condolence (extrapolations)

---

## TIER 1

### Rally
**Scenario:** Software team, release due Friday, two blockers left.

> Subject: Friday
>
> We are two bugs away from shipping 3.0 on time. Both are in checkout, and both have to be fixed and verified by Thursday night or we slip a full month, which we cannot afford with the Q4 pipeline we have. Please treat these as the top priority for everyone who can touch the code, whatever you were working on before. If you are blocked on anything, send me a note directly and I will clear it.
>
> Thanks for the push,
> [Your name]

**Notice:** the point is sentence one; exact count and deadline; the stakes in one clause; open door at the end.
**Real echo:** "Please go all out!" (Tesla end-of-quarter email, Jun 2021)

### Cost control
**Scenario:** Restaurant group, margins slipping.

> Subject: Every dollar
>
> Our margin last quarter was 2%. That is thin enough that one bad month wipes it out, so we need to look at every expense, no matter how small, and ask whether it is truly necessary.
>
> Here is why the small stuff matters. We run 40 kitchens and about 600 purchased items. Saving ten cents on an item we use 5,000 times a week is $26,000 a year. Multiply that by hundreds of items and it becomes real money. Most good ideas will be worth 20 cents here and 50 cents there, not a dramatic breakthrough.
>
> Starting this week, every purchase order over $500 needs the regional manager's sign-off, and I will personally review a sample each Friday. This will take a few weeks to get right, so please do not worry if it is messy at first.
>
> It is the only way to keep prices fair for customers and keep every kitchen open. Thank you for taking this seriously.
>
> [Your name]

**Notice:** opens with the number; does the arithmetic out loud; one personal control mechanism; permission to be imperfect; mission in one sentence.
**Real echo:** "a tough Game of Pennies" (Tesla, Dec 2020)

### Culture directive
**Scenario:** Agency drowning in meetings and jargon.

> Subject: Meetings and jargon
>
> Meetings have taken over our week, and the jargon makes it worse. Starting Monday:
>
> - Cancel any recurring meeting that does not have a clear decision to make. If it is not obviously valuable, kill it.
> - Leave any meeting once you can no longer add value. It is not rude to leave. It is rude to make someone stay.
> - No internal acronyms for projects or tools. If a new hire needs a glossary to follow what you said, rewrite it.
> - Talk to anyone in the company directly if that is the fastest way to solve a problem. You do not need your manager's permission.
>
> If a rule here makes no sense in a specific case, use common sense and tell me so I can fix the rule.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

**Notice:** dash-led rules, each with a reason; one flat consequence-free sentence; common-sense release valve.
**Real echo:** "Excessive meetings are the blight of big companies" (Tesla, Apr 2018)

### Layoffs / reorganization (abridged)
**Scenario:** Startup cutting 12% of staff.

> Subject: Company update
>
> Today we are reducing our team by about 12%, or 14 people. This is the hardest decision I have made here, and I want you to hear it directly from me.
>
> We grew faster than our revenue could support, and some roles now overlap. Our burn is higher than we can sustain, and fixing it now keeps the company alive and lets us keep building. Nobody in customer support or core engineering is affected.
>
> To those leaving: thank you for what you built. Each of you will receive [severance terms] and [benefits details], and I will personally help with references and introductions. I am sorry it has come to this.
>
> To those staying: the product roadmap is [one-sentence plan]. I am making this decision now so we never have to make it again.
>
> [Your name]

**Notice:** the number in sentence one; plain reasoning; who is and isn't affected; a sincere line to leavers; a resolve line.
**Real echo:** "I'm deeply grateful for your many contributions to our mission." (Tesla, 2018 and 2024)
**Add:** suggest the user check with HR/legal before sending.

### Policy edict
**Scenario:** Return to the office.

> Subject: Office attendance
>
> Starting [date], everyone is expected in the office a minimum of 40 hours per week. This is less than we ask of the people on our warehouse floor.
>
> If there are exceptional contributors for whom this is impossible, I will review and approve those exceptions personally. The more senior you are, the more visible you should be. I spent most of last year on the floor for exactly that reason.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

> Subject: To be super clear
>
> Everyone means everyone. If you do not show up, we will assume you have resigned.
>
> [Your name]

**Notice:** the number is stated flatly; fairness comparison; exception path through the sender; the follow-up restates the rule more bluntly.
**Real echo:** "If you don't show up, we will assume you have resigned." (Tesla, May 2022)
**Add:** suggest the user check with HR/legal before sending.

### Crisis all-hands
**Scenario:** Hardware startup, key component failing in the field.

> Subject: Serious problem
>
> The battery failure rate is much worse than we reported last week. There is no way to sugarcoat this.
>
> I will be on the production line all night and through the weekend. Unless you have a family emergency, we need everyone on this until it is fixed.
>
> Here is the chain. If we cannot ship reliable units by [date], we cannot fulfill the [customer] order. If we miss that order, we lose roughly [X] of this year's revenue, and we do not have the cash to absorb that. We need a failure rate under [Y]% by [date].
>
> If you have an idea and feel it is not being heard, email me directly.
>
> [Your name]

**Notice:** severity first; the sender shows up before asking others to; consequences as a plain dependency chain; one exact threshold.
**Real echo:** "There is no way to sugarcoat this." (SpaceX, Nov 2021)

## TIER 2

### Terse replies
> Sounds good.
>
> Ok by me.
>
> Fine by me if they don't use this in active messaging.
>
> Will call.

**Notice:** answers only what was asked; one clause of condition when needed.
**Real echo:** "Sounds good" (OpenAI, Mar 2016, a complete reply to an article fact-check)

### Delegation
> Approved. [Name], please make this happen. Let's aim to have the first batch out by the 15th.

**Notice:** approval, then first-name imperative, then a concrete target and a collaborative "Let's".
**Real echo:** "Consider it done!" (OpenAI/Tesla, Jul 2017)

### Decline
> Thanks for the offer, but I can't travel that week. Let's meet when you're next in town. Not urgent.

**Notice:** brief thanks, flat reason, alternative on his terms, urgency downgraded.
**Real echo:** "This is not an urgent matter." (Tesla, Nov 2016)

### Setback and resolve
**Scenario:** Product launch fails.

> Subject: Plan going forward
>
> Today's launch did not go as hoped, and that is a big disappointment. On the plus side, [the one thing that worked] performed perfectly.
>
> The problem was in [component], which caused [effect]. We are still investigating and I will send a note as soon as we understand exactly what happened.
>
> The most important message is that we will not skip a beat. Unit 4 is almost ready, unit 5 is right behind it, and the next version continues on schedule. We have secured [funding/resources] that covers us well past this.
>
> There should be no question that we will get this working. I will never give up, and I mean never.
>
> Thanks for your hard work, and now on to the next one.
> [Your name]

**Notice:** disappointment stated plainly; one honest positive; the root cause in one sentence; next concrete steps; resource reassurance; resolve.
**Real echo:** "I will never give up and I mean never." (SpaceX, Aug 2008)

### Celebration
> Subject: 10,000
>
> We did it!! We shipped 10,000 units this month. What an amazing team. I could not be more proud to work with you.
>
> The level of creativity was mind-blowing. We rebuilt the packing line over a weekend, flew in parts, and found workarounds for problems nobody thought we could solve.
>
> I think we are now the company we set out to be.
>
> Thank you,
> [Your name]

**Notice:** open with the win in his excited voice; specific feats; one understated big line; thanks.
**Real echo:** "We did it!!" (Tesla, Jul 2018)

## TIER 3

### Persuasive rationale memo (abridged)
**Scenario:** Founder explaining why the company won't raise outside capital.

> Subject: Why we're staying independent
>
> I am increasingly concerned that raising outside capital now would pull us away from what we are here to do. If it makes the mission less likely, we should not do it.
>
> Some of you may think funding would be good for the company. It would bring cash, but it would also bring quarterly pressure and a board that wants growth we cannot responsibly deliver. Many companies that took that route stopped building the product they started with.
>
> YES, BUT WE COULD GROW FASTER
>
> Possibly. But growth that costs us our direction is not a win. We have 22 months of runway at our current burn. If we reach [milestone], we will be able to raise on far better terms or not at all.
>
> BEST OF BOTH WORLDS
>
> My goal is to get most of the benefits of being well-funded without the distraction: steady revenue, careful hiring, no short-term panics.
>
> MY RECOMMENDATION
>
> Do not plan around a funding round this year. Plan around reaching [milestone] by [date].
>
> [Your name]

**Notice:** the position stated early; objection labeled in caps and answered; numbers; a clear recommendation.
**Real echo:** "YES, BUT I COULD MAKE MORE MONEY IF WE WERE PUBLIC" (SpaceX, 2013)

### Board / investor
> Subject: Offer
>
> My view is that [Company] will perform better under a single owner for the next few years, for three reasons:
>
> 1. Short-term quarterly pressure is pushing us toward decisions that are suboptimal for the long term.
> 2. Share-price swings are a constant distraction for the team.
> 3. We are more efficient when we are not managing outside expectations.
>
> Unless another bidder makes a better offer, I would ask that this go to a shareholder vote at the earliest opportunity. The offer expires in 30 days. Anyone who prefers to stay as a shareholder is welcome to.
>
> [Your name]

**Notice:** numbered reasoning; a clear process; a deadline; respect for those who choose differently.
**Real echo:** "This offer expires in 30 days." (Tesla, Aug 2018)

### Customer announcement
> Subject: Trying something new
>
> Word of mouth has always been the best thing that happens to us. When I meet customers, many tell me they have convinced friends to buy. We do not pay for endorsements, and I do not like the idea of tricking people into buying something.
>
> Stores are expensive: it costs about $[X] to make a sale through one. If we can amplify word of mouth, we can save that money and give it to you.
>
> So we are trying an experiment. From now until [date], anyone who buys through your link gets $[X] off, and you get $[X] credit. Just for fun, if five friends buy, you get [a perk money can't buy].
>
> I do not know whether this will work, but it is worth trying.
>
> [Your name]

**Notice:** personal opening; honest economics; framed as an experiment; fun perk.
**Real echo:** "So, we are going to try an experiment." (Tesla, Jul 2015)

### Reply to a journalist
**Scenario:** A reporter claims an outage lasted days and was hidden.

> The outage lasted four hours, not days, and we posted a status update within 20 minutes. If you can show me a customer who was told nothing, I would like to see it. Happy to share the timeline.

**Notice:** corrects the claim with facts; offers evidence; no insults. (Placeholders in real use.)
**Real echo:** "we are more likely to discover unicorns dancing in the flame duct." (SpaceX, Sep 2013; don't reuse the image)

### Business outreach
> Hi [Name],
>
> Would it be possible for our team to buy one of the early units of your new system?
>
> Worth flagging: we are unaffiliated with [other company]. We are a small research group and want to be sure that is clear.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

**Notice:** direct ask first; one "worth flagging" line to prevent a misunderstanding.
**Real echo:** "Worth flagging: OpenAI is unaffiliated with Tesla." (Apr 2016)

## TIER 4 (extrapolations: add the extrapolation note)

### Apology
> Subject: What happened and what changes
>
> I got this wrong. [One sentence on what happened.] That is on me, not the team.
>
> Starting [date], we will [specific fix]. I will check on it myself every [interval] until it is done. If you see it happen again, email me directly.
>
> I am sorry.
> [Your name]

**Notice:** plain accountability, concrete fix, a date, no groveling. The corpus has almost no apologies, so this is an extrapolation.

### Condolence
> I am so sorry about your father. Please take whatever time you need, and do not worry about work. If there is anything I can do, tell me.
>
> [Your name]

**Notice:** all intensity removed; short, sincere, no humor or mission talk. This is an extrapolation.
