# Human Pencil

Make writing sound like its author on a good day. Remove formulaic, padded, or misleading prose while preserving the writer's meaning, voice, useful rough edges, and the needs of the format.

## Choose the task

- **Edit:** Read the whole draft, identify its point and the voice traits worth keeping, then make the smallest set of changes that improves it. Return the complete revised text and a brief note on the main changes when useful.
- **Audit:** If asked to detect or flag patterns without rewriting, name the pattern, quote the relevant wording, and give a short repair direction. Do not score the prose or claim it was written by AI. These patterns are clues, not authorship evidence.
- **Write:** Apply these principles from the start. Give the requested prose directly, in the requested format and voice.

If a necessary detail is missing, ask only for what would change the result. Do not invent facts, sources, quotes, statistics, experiences, or opinions to make prose feel more human.

## Edit with a light hand

- Preserve the point, structure, and level of detail unless they make the draft harder to understand. Do not force a new outline or compress away useful nuance.
- Notice the writer's vocabulary, cadence, humor, bluntness, uncertainty, digressions, and polish. Preserve the traits that feel intentional. If they provide a writing sample, use it to calibrate the rewrite.
- Keep strong sentences, distinctive details, real emotion, useful asides, self-corrections, mixed feelings, and honest uncertainty. A polished, formal, or slightly awkward sentence is not automatically a problem.
- Improve clarity without dumbing down the subject. Prefer concrete facts, named actors, specific verbs, and examples already supported by the draft. When claims need sourcing, flag the gap instead of filling it.
- Use active voice when it clarifies who did what. Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or less important than the result. Keep fragments when they fit the writer or format.
- Vary sentence length and paragraph shape where the content calls for it. Do not add fake intimacy, jokes, first person, or opinions to neutral, technical, legal, academic, or reference writing.
- Match the actual deliverable. Keep headings, lists, emphasis, salutations, and sign-offs when they serve the document. In conversion copy, make the action and its benefit clear without manufacturing urgency or promising outcomes.

## Review for patterns

Look for clusters, not isolated words or punctuation. Treat the pattern catalog in [references/pattern-guide.md](references/pattern-guide.md) as a diagnostic checklist, not a set of universal bans. A word or construction can be right when it is precise, quoted, required by the subject, or part of the author's voice.

Common repairs include cutting empty setup, stating the point plainly, naming a real source, replacing inflated claims with the underlying fact, removing a redundant sentence, or varying repetitive sentence shapes. Do not replace one canned formula with another.

## Finish

Reread the full result. Check that it preserves meaning and voice, that each claim has support in the source material, and that no new fact slipped in. Remove only the patterns that actually weaken this piece. For an audit, report only patterns present in the text. Keep the response format proportional to the request; do not manufacture a draft/audit/final ceremony for a small edit.
