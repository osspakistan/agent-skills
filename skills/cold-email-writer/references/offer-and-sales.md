# Offer and sales layer (Hormozi)

Use for sales, service, agency, and customer outreach, and any time the email's weak point is the offer rather than the wording. For investor or advice asks, skip most of this.

## Core idea
A well-written email can't rescue a vague offer. "Buy my thing" gets ignored. Make sure there is an actual offer before polishing the prose.

## How it maps onto the email
| Hormozi | In the email |
|---|---|
| Sell the outcome | Lead with the result, not features or the product category |
| Pain is the pitch | Name their pain accurately, in their words, in line 1 or 2 |
| Nail the hook first | First line must make them notice; the hook is a specific pain or result, never flattery |
| Raise perceived likelihood | One proof point, guarantee, or a one-line mechanism (how it works) |
| Shrink time delay | State the first win timeline ("first results in 7 days") |
| Cut effort and sacrifice | Say what you do for them ("done for you", "we set it up") |
| Niche until it feels too narrow | Name the specific segment ("for dental clinics with 3+ locations") |
| Itemize every bonus | If adding bonuses, list each with its value, never "plus extras" |
| Find a starving crowd | Target people already feeling the pain; pick recipients by hunger, not by list size |
| Sell richer customers first | Prioritize recipients who can pay and have budget |

## Value equation (diagnostic)
Value rises with **dream outcome** and **perceived likelihood**, falls with **time delay** and **effort/sacrifice**. If a draft is weak, find which of the four is missing and add one line for it.

## Rules that apply to cold email
- **Offer, then ask.** State the offer in one or two lines: outcome + mechanism + timeline. Then a small ask.
- **Guarantee only if real.** A risk-reversal line ("no result, no charge") raises likelihood but must be something the sender will honor.
- **Scarcity and urgency only if true** (limited slots, round closing date). Fake urgency damages trust.
- **Name the offer** if it's a package, so it's memorable.
- **Don't discount to get replies.** Price cuts attract cheap clients; fix the offer instead.

## Rules for the sales process around the email
Not email copy, but mention them when the user's problem is "emails get replies but don't close":
1. Have a real offer, not "buy my thing".
2. Have a written script for the call/reply.
3. Practice it daily; stay on script.
4. **Respond to leads immediately.** Speed to contact is a close-rate lever. Prepare reply templates for common responses (interested, price?, not now).
5. Sell to richer customers.
- If close rate is high, raise price. Price premium or lowest; the middle gets killed.
- Offer sequence after the first yes: attraction offer, then upsell, downsell, continuity.
- Cash from the first 30 days should cover acquiring the next two customers.

## Reconciling with YC/OpenVC
| Tension | Resolution |
|---|---|
| "Nail the hook" vs "skip the hook / no flattery" | Different hooks. Drop the personalized flattery hook. Keep a *pain/outcome* hook as the first line. |
| Bonus stacking, urgency vs "scarcity as fact" | Only stack what's real and itemized; scarcity must be true. |
| "Grand Slam Offer" vs "3 sentences" | The offer must be compressible: outcome, mechanism, timeline in 1-2 sentences. Full details go in the reply or a linked page, not the cold email. |
| Selling vs YC "don't ask for a meeting" | Offer something small: a short breakdown, audit, or demo, not a call. |

## Example (offer-led cold email)
> Subject: Fewer no-shows at [Clinic]
>
> Hi Dr. Patel,
>
> Multi-location dental clinics lose ~15% of appointments to no-shows. We cut that in half within 30 days with automated reminders we set up for you [client name, result].
>
> If a no-show audit of your last 3 months would help, I'll send one free this week.
>
> Sam
