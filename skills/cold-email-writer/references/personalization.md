# Personalization

## Why the old hook broke
A year or two ago, "Heard you on the podcast, congrats on X, since you're big on Y" signaled homework. Now AI and mail-merge produce it at scale, so it signals a template with a scraped fact. Recipients (investors especially) see dozens a week. Fake personalization is now worse than none.

## The swap test
Could this exact sentence be pasted into an email to a different recipient with only a name change? If yes, cut it.

**Fails**: congratulating a recent deal, "you're big on vertical SaaS", "I saw you liked a post about fintech", praising a podcast.
**Passes**: a specific thesis they wrote that your company is a direct instance of; a portfolio gap you fill; a customer or mutual contact they'd recognize; a problem they described publicly, in their words; a genuine shared background that is relevant ("uncommon commonality").

## How to include a real one
- One sentence max, and it must connect to the ask or the proof ("You wrote that bank QA is still manual. We automate it; 2 paid pilots live.").
- Put it after what you do or fused with it, never as a flattering opener.
- No compliments. Reference the substance, not the person.

## When there's no real reason
Skip it. Lead with product and proof. A clean no-hook email beats a fake-hook email. At volume (e.g. 20 a day) accept lighter personalization but keep it honest, or segment so each group gets a true, relevant line.

## Targeting beats personalizing
100 good-fit recipients with a clear email outperform 1,000 random ones with a clever hook. If you can't find a real reason it's them, question whether it's the right recipient.
